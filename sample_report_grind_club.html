Findings

  • Across 10,000 simulated futures, Grind Club survives the full 30-month horizon in 60% of them.
  • When it fails, the median time of death is month 10, and 95% of failures happen in the first year.
  • The number one killer is customer acquisition cost: it is the decisive factor in 63% of failures.
  • If customer acquisition cost lands in its worst 10% ($95), survival falls to 13%; in its best 10% ($49) it rises to 99%.
  • To reach 80% survival, customer acquisition cost must be at most $61. Only 33% of the range you assumed clears that bar.
  • The strongest decision tested is “Referral program (organic +50%)”, which moves survival from 60% to 77% (+17 points).
  • Median LTV to CAC is 2.0x (a common benchmark is 3x or more) and CAC is paid back in about 5 months.
